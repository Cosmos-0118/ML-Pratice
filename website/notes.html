  • !What did not work
Logistic regression is not beaten. Rain tomorrow depends on today's weather in a fairly simple way, so trees, boosting and stacking have little to add. The best models gain only about 3 points over "tomorrow = today".
  • A hidden Markov model finds clear wet and dry spells, but it predicts tomorrow's rain no better than "tomorrow = today" (68.7% vs 70.3%).
  • The best single tree is just one question deep. Deeper trees fit the past and do worse on the next year.
  • Clustering has no clear number of groups: silhouette is modest for every k and BIC keeps falling. k = 4 is a readability choice, and the three methods agree only partly.
  • The test year has only 364 days, so one accuracy figure is uncertain by about ±2.4 points. Differences smaller than that should not be trusted.
  • The dataset's weather label (sun, rain, fog, ...) was built by its authors from the other columns, so it is never used as an input. It is only used afterwards to check the clusters.
